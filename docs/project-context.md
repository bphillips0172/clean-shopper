## 1. Problem Statement
Parents with children and pets want to replace toxic products in their homes — cleaning supplies, personal care items, pantry staples — but the research is overwhelming and unreliable. Product labels require expert knowledge to interpret, "clean" and "natural" claims are unverified, and there is no single place to do this research or remember what you've learned.

## 2. ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)
A parent or caregiver with children and/or pets who is actively trying to reduce their household's exposure to harmful chemicals. They are already motivated — they've started reading labels, Googling ingredients, or checking EWG — but find the process fragmented and exhausting. They don't have the ingredient expertise to evaluate what they find, and they repeat the same research every time they need to buy something new. They have accumulated opinions (brands they trust, ingredients they avoid) but no system to store them.

## 3. Pain Points
- Ingredient lists require domain expertise to evaluate; most users can't tell a safe ingredient from a harmful one by name
- "Clean," "natural," and "non-toxic" on packaging are marketing terms with no standard definition, making them useless as signals
- Research is spread across multiple sources (EWG, brand sites, Reddit threads) with no way to compare or consolidate findings
- Preferences and knowledge built up over time — trusted brands, avoided ingredients, certifications that matter — live only in memory and must be re-derived every time

## 4. Proposed Solution
Users can:
- Describe what they're looking for in plain language and receive ranked product recommendations with ingredient-level reasoning
- Save preferences — ingredients to avoid, trusted brands, certifications that matter — and have those applied automatically to every future recommendation
- Compare products side by side and receive a clear recommendation filtered through their saved preferences
- Save products to a persistent shopping list organized by home, personal care, and pantry categories

## 8. Gaps

2. **Platform decision** — The brief explicitly defers this to the designer. This is the single most consequential design decision and needs to be made before any UI work begins.
3. **Data persistence mechanism** — Authentication is out of scope for V1, but preferences and shopping lists need to persist. The technical approach here directly shapes the UX (anonymous sessions, local storage, device-tied data, etc.).
4. **"Clean" definition** — The product's core value depends on a defensible, consistent standard for ingredient safety. The brief references EWG but does not specify how scores map to recommendations.
5. **Geography and localization** — No mention of where this launches, which affects product availability, regulatory context, and data sources.
